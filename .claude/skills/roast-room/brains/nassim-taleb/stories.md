# Stories: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Stories and cases he reuses, from 80 public sources (2004 to 2026). One or two lines each, with the lesson and source ids.

1. **The casino: 100 gamblers versus one gambler for 100 days.** The house can average across people; you cannot average across your own ruin. Lesson: ensemble probability is not time probability. (S020, S007, S055)
2. **Russian roulette with a positive expected return.** Five out of six times you win; it is still irrational. Lesson: expected value hides ruin. (S055, S051)
3. **The firm short volatility in fall 2017.** It forecast volatility correctly and went bust because the move came in one lump. Lesson: better be convex than right. (S020)
4. **Long Term Capital Management, 1998.** Nobel laureates called it a "10 sigma" event; about sixty finance PhDs blew up on similar trades. Lesson: a 10 sigma event means your model is wrong (Wittgenstein's ruler). (S020, S043, S062)
5. **Option sellers who blew up.** Selling gold options for five cents, buying them back at forty dollars; Niederhoffer. Lesson: steady income from selling tails ends in one blow. (S053)
6. **"Please tell me to whom, sir?"** A forced liquidation in a market with no buyers. Lesson: you cannot sell what nobody will buy at the moment you must sell. (S053)
7. **The turkey and the subprime package.** A friend was sold leveraged subprime loans because thirty years of history showed the trade was safe. Lesson: the past is the turkey's data before Thanksgiving. (S043)
8. **Bear Stearns funds that never had a down month.** Investors rushed in for low volatility; then they blew up. Lesson: fake low volatility. (S075)
9. **Fannie Mae, 2003.** He said its losses would accelerate as rates rose; it blew up five years later. Lesson: accelerating losses reveal fragility before the event. (S055, S075)
10. **CalPERS and Universa, 2019 to 2020.** The pension dropped its tail hedge in late 2019 as too costly and missed about a billion dollars of gains in March 2020. Lesson: judge a hedge by the whole portfolio, including what the alternatives lost. (S083)
11. **Universa's competitors.** They bought cheaper puts on other indexes that "should" correlate; one went bust. Lesson: no correlation substitutes in a hedge. (S053)
12. **Two brothers, one borrows.** Same forecast, same error; the borrower goes bust, the equity issuer survives. Lesson: debt turns error into ruin. (S072, S056)
13. **Forest fires and Greenspan.** Suppressing every small fire builds fuel for the big one; Greenspan cut rates at every wobble. Lesson: suppressed volatility builds fragility. (S075, S077)
14. **Syria and Italy.** Syria was calm for 40 years, then collapsed; Italy is in constant turmoil and stable. Lesson: low volatility is not low risk. (S075, S085)
15. **Robert Rubin's 120 million dollars.** He kept his Citibank pay; taxpayers took the loss. Lesson: the Bob Rubin trade, upside without downside. (S052, S056, S079)
16. **Lindy's restaurant.** Went bankrupt the day his book on the Lindy effect came out; Broadway actors there noticed old shows outlast new ones. Lesson: what has survived tends to survive. (S052, S054)
17. **Pink champagne the day Lehman went bust.** He and Patterson celebrated the end of a fragile bank. Lesson: tail bets pay rarely and hugely. (S053)
18. **Fat Tony versus Nero.** The street-smart trader smells the sucker; the theorist needs graphs. Lesson: practitioners detect fragility without models. (S078, S001)
19. **Saint Marina.** A local saint who bore an unjust accusation for a decade without defending herself. Lesson: dull pain is harder than acute pain; tail hedgers must live without vindication. (S062)
20. **Two kidneys.** The spare lets you stop predicting the failure of the first. Lesson: redundancy is an option. (S076, S056)
