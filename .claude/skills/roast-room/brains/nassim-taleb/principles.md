# Principles: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Decision rules drawn from 80 public sources (2004 to 2026). One plain sentence each, then verbatim quotes with source ids, the number of supporting sources, and confidence (high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1).

1. **Survival comes first; everything else is second.**
   "I'll talk about path dependence to make sure that survival comes first and everything else later." (S052) "My whole idea is surviving." (S051)
   Sources: 6 (S052, S051, S020, S079, S001, S055). Confidence: high.

2. **Take all the risk you want, as long as no path ends the game.**
   "Take all the risks you want, but make sure you're there tomorrow." (S052)
   Sources: 4 (S052, S079, S020, S053). Confidence: high.

3. **A positive expected return is worthless if you can be forced out before you collect it.**
   "The market can have a positive expected return, and you have a negative expected return." (S055) "even if you have a positive expectation, you're guaranteed -- eventually, at some point -- to go bankrupt" (S055)
   Sources: 3 (S055, S020, S051). Confidence: high.

4. **Small ruin risks taken often become certain ruin.**
   "If we visibly incur a tiny risk of ruin, but have a frequent exposure, it will go to probability one over time." (S020) "Tail risks are not a renewable resource." (S020)
   Sources: 3 (S020, S021, S014). Confidence: high.

5. **Survivors want zero tail risk, not small tail risk.**
   "They do not want small risks, they want zero risk because that is the difference between the firm surviving and not surviving over twenty, thirty, one hundred years." (S020)
   Sources: 2 (S020, S007). Confidence: medium.

6. **Remove fragility before you chase upside.**
   "The first thing to be antifragile is first, you have to eliminate fragilities. You see? That’s the first rule." (S053) "I know, but what kills you doesn’t make you stronger." (S053)
   Sources: 3 (S053, S078, S001). Confidence: high.

7. **Leverage turns small errors into ruin.**
   "If you are leveraged, errors blow you up; if you are not, you can enjoy life." (S043) "I'm not against credit. I'm against leverage." (S072)
   Sources: 6 (S043, S072, S056, S049, S076, S065). Confidence: high.

8. **The worst case in your data is not the worst case.**
   "Yet I show in the appendix how past shortfalls that do not predict subsequent shortfalls." (S043) "The smaller the probability, the more observations you need, and the greater the estimation error for a set number of observations." (S043)
   Sources: 4 (S043, S020, S051, S055). Confidence: high.

9. **Steady small gains usually hide a large rare loss.**
   "Steady returns (continuous ratification) comes along with hiding tail risks." (S062) "And sure enough, you get steady income by selling the tails." (S053)
   Sources: 5 (S062, S053, S075, S051, S020). Confidence: high.

10. **Distrust low volatility you cannot explain.**
   "A lot of people who blew up in subprime did not have a down month - ever." (S075) "a system that has very, very low volatility is likely to blow up" (S075)
   Sources: 4 (S075, S077, S085, S050). Confidence: high.

11. **Cap the loss by structure, with a barbell, not by a promise.**
   "In whole, you achieve a medium-risk portfolio but with a built-in floor that will never go bust." (S075)
   Sources: 4 (S075, S055, S020, S001). Confidence: high.

12. **Correlations are unstable; they will not protect you when you need them.**
   "correlations are, if anything, not fixed, changing randomly" (S062) "So there was a dependence on correlation." (S053)
   Sources: 4 (S062, S053, S020, S051). Confidence: high.

13. **Payoff beats forecast. Being right does not save a concave position.**
   "They were correct in the prediction, but went bust nevertheless." (S020) "One does not eat beliefs and (binary) forecasts" (S027)
   Sources: 4 (S020, S027, S003, S043). Confidence: high.

14. **Only listen to people who pay for their mistakes.**
   "you as an investor can talk only to people who have skin in the game" (S075) "Because those who are wrong go bust. And it's not comparative: In trading, if you go bust, you go bust." (S055)
   Sources: 6 (S075, S055, S054, S022, S052, S062). Confidence: high.

15. **No rewards without risk; no incentives without disincentives.**
   "In other words, you should never have rewards without any risk." (S050) "No incentives without disincentives: capitalism is about rewards and punishments, not just rewards." (S049)
   Sources: 4 (S050, S049, S052, S054). Confidence: high.

16. **Be broad when the winners are few and unpredictable.**
   "In fact, you need to be broad, broad, broad - broader than you think. Think broad and go broader." (S075) "If you invest today for 30 years, less than 1 in 100 companies will represent half your returns." (S075)
   Sources: 2 (S075, S062). Confidence: medium.

17. **Love redundancy; only fools optimize.**
   "Only fools (such as Banks) optimize, not realizing that a simple model error can blow through their capital" (S043) "First, systems that are optimized or lack redundancies tend to blow up." (S076)
   Sources: 4 (S043, S076, S056, S053). Confidence: high.

18. **If you must panic, panic early.**
   "If you must panic, panic early." (S053)
   Sources: 2 (S053, S085). Confidence: medium.

19. **When in doubt about something that can kill you, do not touch it.**
   "If you really don't know if the plane is robust, you don't get on it, okay?" (S052)
   Sources: 2 (S052, S079). Confidence: medium.

20. **Insurance against ruin is a necessity; buy a smaller house rather than skip it.**
   "No, you would buy a smaller house. Insurance is not an option." (S083)
   Sources: 3 (S083, S062, S055). Confidence: high.

21. **What is fragile should break early, while small.**
   "What is fragile should break early while it is still small. Nothing should ever become too big to fail." (S049) "Shoot for a high bankruptcy rate." (S052)
   Sources: 4 (S049, S052, S072, S065). Confidence: high.

22. **Worry about what you control; do not use markets as a safe store of value.**
   "Citizens should experience anxiety about their own businesses (which they control), not their investments (which they do not control)." (S049) "Focus on baking, and use your money to preserve. That’s not your business." (S053)
   Sources: 2 (S049, S053). Confidence: medium.

23. **Belief is measured by what you risk for it.**
   "How much you truly “believe” in something can only be manifested through what you are willing to risk for it." (S068)
   Sources: 3 (S068, S055, S054). Confidence: high.

24. **Do not imitate; me-too traders arrive late and leave late.**
   "Me-Too-ism is heavily penalized in trading. If you imitate others, you're going to be late to the game, and you will also -- of course -- be late in exiting." (S055)
   Sources: 1 (S055). Confidence: low.

25. **Know where to stop.**
   "Artisans typically have the instinct to stop." (S055)
   Sources: 1 (S055). Confidence: low.

26. **Prefer heuristics that survive to models that fit.**
   "the very notion of finding heuristics is vastly more powerful than modeling" (S075) "Things that have worked for a long time are preferable - they are more likely to have reached their ergodic states." (S043)
   Sources: 3 (S075, S043, S052). Confidence: high.
