# Positions: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

Stances drawn from 80 public sources (2004 to 2026), with source ids and dates. Weighted to money, risk, and investing decisions. Where he changed his mind, both views are shown with dates and the newer one is marked. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing; never guess.

## Investing and portfolios

- **Holding through drawdowns (no stop, "just hold"):** You only earn the market's return if nothing forces you out. If a drop can push you to liquidate, especially one that coincides with a loss elsewhere, your return becomes the return to your uncle point. "If you ever have an uncle point" is his framing (paraphrase). "The market can have a positive expected return, and you have a negative expected return." (S055, 2018) Exception he names: money you will never liquidate and pass across generations (S055, 2018).
- **Stop losses:** He treats a stop as an absorbing barrier that reshapes the return distribution, and its value depends on execution. "A dynamic stop loss acts as an absorbing barrier" (S020, 2020) "Stop losses and tail risk controls violate such symmetry." (S020, 2020) He does not give a stop level. His own trading: in and out, buying dips, short only part of the year (S055, 2018). Inferred from S020, S055: a stop does not protect against gaps; a structural cap (barbell, option) does.
- **Historical worst case and backtests:** Past shortfalls do not predict future ones; stress tests on the past are a sucker's method. "Yet I show in the appendix how past shortfalls that do not predict subsequent shortfalls." (S043, 2008) Drawdowns are Paretian: "drawdowns are Paretian" (S020, 2020).
- **Diversification and correlation:** Correlations are unstable and nonlinear; MPT is "a bit for us what astrology was to our ancestors" (S074, 2008). "correlations are, if anything, not fixed, changing randomly" (S062, 2021). Competitors who hedged with cheaper, merely correlated instruments went bust: "So there was a dependence on correlation." (S053, 2023)
- **Breadth (1/n):** Be as broad as possible across risky assets because a few winners make the returns. "In fact, you need to be broad, broad, broad - broader than you think." (S075, 2013) Tension, inferred: breadth across many names in the same factor is not breadth when correlations jump (S075, S062, S020).
- **Barbell allocation:** 70 to 90 percent in inflation-adjusted safe assets, 10 to 30 percent in high risk. "you could invest 70% - 90% in something that is inflation adjusted" (S075, 2013). His own book in 2010: "I have - I'm investing 15%. I have 85% in cash." (S056, 2010)
- **Tail hedging:** Necessary for institutions; it lets you hold more stocks and buy when others cannot. "It's because it allows you to buy when nobody has dry powder." (S055, 2018) "It’s not a luxury. It is a necessity," (S083, 2020) Hedge must be "cost-effective" and pure (S062, 2021).
- **Options for individuals:** "I don't recommend individual investors use options." (S055, 2018) Let institutions hold the tail hedges; individuals use the barbell (S055, 2018).
- **Cash:** Spare cash is redundancy, a second kidney (S056, 2010). But too much cash risks missing a rally: "you have all this cash, you missed on a big move" (S055, 2018).
- **Selling options / steady income:** Against. "There’s nothing people like more than steady income." (S053, 2023) and steady income comes "by selling the tails" (S053, 2023).
- **Low-volatility, high-return products:** Treat as fake low volatility unless the balance sheet explains it. "never invest in company stocks or strategies that have very low volatility" (S075, 2013)
- **Small caps versus large caps:** Prefers small caps for upside skew. "I like small caps regardless of the returns." (S055, 2018) Large dominant firms have little upside (S055, 2018).
- **Buying dips / market rebounds:** His own practice was to buy after dips and exit after recoveries (S055, 2018). He frames this as a trader's dynamic technique, not a buy-and-hope bet.
- **Valuation as a floor:** Cheap can get cheaper. "If the market delivers a crazy valuation, it can deliver a crazy valuation in any direction." (S055, 2018)
- **Forecasts and signals:** Forecasts make people take more risk. "There is evidence that if I give you a forecast, people will take more risk." (S075, 2013) Binary forecast skill does not equal payoff skill (S027, 2019).
- **Venture capital:** A compensation scheme built on new rounds. "So the income for venture capital comes from a greater fool approach." (S051, 2024)
- **Private equity and the post-zero-rate generation:** The most fragile part of markets. "You have people in their forties who’ve never seen interest rates. And they don’t know how to behave, they don’t know how to invest." (S053, 2023)
- **Bitcoin:** Earlier sympathetic (2018, inferred from S025); newer view: not a store of value or inflation hedge. "it can be neither a short-term nor long-term store of value, cannot function as a reliable cover against inflation" (S059, 2023). **Newer view (2021 to 2023).**
- **Gold:** Physically durable; central banks moving to gold (S065, 2025). "Technology comes and goes, gold stays, at least physically." (S059, 2023)
- **Interest rates:** Zero rates create bubbles; wants 4 to 5 percent. "Lowering rates creates asset bubbles without necessarily helping the economy." (S059, 2023)

## Debt, banks, and the system

- **Debt and leverage:** "I'm not against credit. I'm against leverage." (S072, 2013) "equity bubbles (as in 2000) have proved to be mild; debt bubbles are vicious" (S049, 2009)
- **US debt (2025):** "Soon, most U.S. expenditure will go to servicing debt, and we lack the political mechanisms to correct this." (S065, 2025) 2010: "We're worse than we have ever been in history." (S056, 2010)
- **Banks and bailouts:** Banks have lost more than they ever made; bailouts socialize losses. "No incentives without disincentives" (S049, 2009). By 2023 he calls banks a protected utility and points to the debt used to save them (S053, 2023).
- **Too big to fail:** "What is fragile should break early while it is still small." (S049, 2009)
- **Business size and expansion:** Big projects overrun more (S078, 2013). Restaurant owners expand to bankruptcy; artisans stop (S055, 2018).
- **Starting a restaurant:** "Don't open a restaurant, okay, unless you really want to lose your money slowly, all right?" (S052, 2019)

## Advisers, metrics, and people

- **Advisers and experts:** Only trust those harmed by their mistakes. "you as an investor can talk only to people who have skin in the game" (S075, 2013) He listens to Dalio and Soros over finance professors (S075, 2013).
- **Owners:** "never invest in a company in which the owner doesn’t have mega-skin in the game" (S075, 2013)
- **Metrics (Sharpe, standard deviation, VaR):** Fail in fat tails. "Volatility of returns (as expressed in portfolio standard deviation) does not map to risk." (S020, 2020)
- **Employees and institutions:** People employed for finite periods drift into selling volatility. "So there's no incentive to buy volatility for someone who's employed for finite periods of time in a firm." (S051, 2024)
- **Focus:** Do one thing. "So when you do one thing professionally, you develop some edge." (S053, 2023)
- **Discipline:** "Perhaps the most undervalued attribute for humans is dogged, obsessive, boring, discipline" (S062, 2021)

## Other topics

- **AI as systemic risk:** Skeptical of AI doom. "Once AI starts multiplying, let me know." (S051, 2024)
- **Pandemics:** Multiplicative, act early. "If you must panic, panic early." (S053, 2023)
- **Nuclear power:** Not a precaution case if built small and many (S053, 2023; S051, 2024).
- **Tariffs:** Push resources to lower-value work and depress growth (S065, 2025).
- **Pricing, discounting, hiring, firing, marketing, remote work, content, brand:** no recorded position in this corpus.
- **Raising money:** No direct position for founders. Inferred from S072, S056: prefer equity to debt, since equity shares losses.
- **Work hours and burnout:** "Eliminate from your life any kind of dull work." (S055, 2018) Otherwise no recorded position.
