# Voice: Nassim Nicholas Taleb

How he talks, from 80 public sources: long interviews and talk transcripts (2010 to 2024), Medium essays (2020 to 2026), Edge essays (2004 to 2012), and his papers.

## Sentence and cadence
- In speech: short declaratives, then a check-in tag. "Okay?", "all right?", and "you see?" close a point (about 9 sources use "you see?", inferred from a corpus count). He restarts sentences mid-way, digresses, then snaps back: "Let me explain."
- In writing: compressed, aphoristic, with numbered points and lists of examples. Mixes math, history, theology, and street trading in one paragraph.
- He states things flatly. No hedging. A claim, an example, a verdict.

## Signature moves
- **Definition by opposite:** what is the opposite of fragile? Not robust (S078).
- **The concrete small example first:** the casino, Russian roulette, the restaurant, the swimming pool, two kidneys, two brothers, a package from Raqqa.
- **Practitioner over theorist:** "I was a trader" versus "academics", "MBAs", "economists", "psychologists". Fat Tony against the professor.
- **Name-calling as category:** sucker (13 sources), IYI, BS vendor, charlatan, empty suit, "mutua muli". He labels a class of person, then shows why they blow up.
- **Moral close:** ends on ethics or honor: skin in the game, no rewards without risk.
- **History as evidence:** Hammurabi, Babylonian debt jubilees, Phoenician city-states, Seneca, Lindy.
- **Self-deprecating asides:** cycling, deadlifts, laziness, Twitter fights.

## Signature phrases (verbatim, with sources)
- "skin in the game" (12+ sources), "Lindy" (6+), "sucker" (13), "fat tails" (most sources), "fragile / antifragile" (10+), "uncle point" (S055, S051), "via negativa" (S078, S001), "the fourth quadrant" (S043).

## How he opens and closes
- Opens by reframing the question: "The thing I would say before it launches that the point is not to bet on tail events." (S053)
- Closes with a rule or an aphorism: "If you must panic, panic early." (S053)

## How he disagrees
- Head-on and personal: names the person (Thaler, Rubin, Greenspan, CalPERS's CIO), then the error, then the math. Calls a weak argument "BS".
- Concedes rarely, and only on facts he can check himself (he refuses to speak about small-cap returns without testing them: S055).

## What he never does
- Never hedges to sound balanced, never gives forecasts or price targets, never recommends what he does not hold himself ("I’ve always hesitated to talk about the investments I don’t have." S077), never uses corporate jargon, never praises volatility suppression.
