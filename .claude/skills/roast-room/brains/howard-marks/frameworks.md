# Frameworks: Howard Marks

Built from 134 public sources (Oaktree client memos 1990 to 2026 plus long interviews and talks, 739k words); see sources.md. Each entry: what it is, the parts, when to use it, how to score a decision with it (guidance drawn from his own thresholds, labeled as guidance), one verbatim quote, and how many sources in the corpus use it. Source counts are distinct sources whose text uses the idea, from a full-text search of the corpus. Most-used first.

## 1. The pendulum of investor psychology
- **What:** Investor mood swings between greed and fear, euphoria and depression, credulity and skepticism, risk tolerance and risk aversion. It spends almost no time at the midpoint, and an extreme is followed by a swing back.
- **Parts:** where the pendulum is now; which way it is moving; how far from the midpoint; what the extreme is doing to prices.
- **When:** any decision whose payoff depends on what other investors will pay later.
- **Score (guidance):** Name the current mood in one sentence with evidence (deal terms, headlines, flows, what people say at dinner). Near the fearful extreme, lean toward buying; near the greedy extreme, lean toward defense. In the middle, the pendulum tells you little (inferred from S021, S049, S058).
- **Quote:** "Instead, it is almost always swinging toward or away from the extremes of its arc." (S049)
- **Sources:** 27 (for example S004, S005, S013, S040, S049, S057, S058, S063, S073, S083).

## 2. Know where we are (you can't predict, you can prepare)
- **What:** The future cannot be forecast, but the present can be read. Knowing where we stand in the cycle tilts the odds without predicting the timing, size, or shape of the next move.
- **Parts:** reject macro forecasts as a basis for action; assess the present (valuations, psychology, credit terms); prepare for a range of outcomes rather than one.
- **When:** whenever someone wants to act on a forecast of the economy, rates, or the market.
- **Score (guidance):** If the case rests on a forecast that the market will rise or rates will fall, mark it down. If it rests on what is true today (prices, sentiment, terms), mark it up (inferred from S107, S068, S027).
- **Quote:** "Knowing where you are in a cycle and what that implies for the future is very different from predicting the timing, extent and shape of the next cyclical move." (S107)
- **Sources:** 18 on cycle position (S004, S017, S057, S068, S091, S100, S107, S129); 41 sources reject forecasting.

## 3. Taking the temperature of the market
- **What:** His working method for reading cycle position. It is observation and judgment, not data: is the market heated or frigid?
- **Parts:** (1) pattern recognition from market history; (2) cycles as excesses and corrections; (3) watch for "things can only get better" or "only get worse" thinking; (4) be contrarian only at extremes, and know what the herd is doing, why, what is wrong with it, and what to do instead; (5) emotions drive markets; (6) resist your own emotion; (7) act on illogical propositions.
- **When:** before a market-level call, and before trusting any signal that says the market is cheap or dear.
- **Score (guidance):** Count how many of the seven components point the same way. He made only five market calls in about 50 years, so a reading should be extreme before it drives action (S021).
- **Quote:** "it has more to do with clear-eyed observations and assessments of the implications of what you see than with computers, financial data, or calculations" (S021)
- **Sources:** 9 by name (S006, S007, S009, S021, S041, S045, S057, S129, S138).

## 4. Price versus value (it's what you pay)
- **What:** No asset is so good it cannot be overpriced, and none so bad it cannot be cheap enough. Success comes from buying things well, not buying good things.
- **Parts:** a strongly held estimate of intrinsic value based on earning power; today's price; the gap; how much optimism is in the price.
- **When:** every purchase.
- **Score (guidance):** Write value and price side by side. A quality story with no view of value scores zero. A low price with horrible fundamentals is not a bargain either; he wants "some combination of excellence of quality and/or attractive price" (S138).
- **Quote:** "We think no asset is so bad that there's not a price at which it's attractive for purchase, and no asset is so good that it can't be overpriced." (S118)
- **Sources:** 18 (S015, S022, S060, S084, S086, S095, S100, S105, S118, S125, S129, S138); 47 discuss intrinsic value.

## 5. Skepticism is not pessimism (the limits to negativism)
- **What:** Skepticism means doubting what everyone believes. In a boom it calls for pessimism; in a panic it calls for optimism. If the bad story is already in the price, the larger gain lies in the story few believe.
- **Parts:** what everyone believes; whether it is priced in; what you make if it is wrong.
- **When:** in a sell-off, a crash, or a period when no scenario seems too negative.
- **Score (guidance):** If you cannot find anyone who will grant "it can't be that bad", the odds favor buying (inferred from S021, S073).
- **Quote:** "Skepticism calls for pessimism when optimism is excessive. But it also calls for optimism when pessimism is excessive" (S073)
- **Sources:** 49 use skepticism; the 2008 epiphany is retold in S021, S065, S072, S073, S134.

## 6. Risk means permanent loss, not volatility
- **What:** Risk is the chance of a bad outcome, above all a loss you cannot recover. Volatility is only a symptom. The riskiest thing is overpaying, and the best risk reducer is a low price.
- **Parts:** what can go permanently wrong; whether you can be forced to sell; price paid; leverage.
- **When:** sizing a position, judging a "safe" asset, or reacting to a drawdown.
- **Score (guidance):** Ask whether a decline is temporary (hold or buy) or permanent (sell). Forced selling, by margin, redemptions, or nerve, turns the first into the second (S048, S105).
- **Quote:** "We can ride out volatility, but we never get a chance to undo a permanent loss." (S054)
- **Sources:** 12 (S002, S028, S048, S051, S054, S055, S072, S092, S105).

## 7. The riskiest thing is the belief that there's no risk (perversity of risk)
- **What:** When people think risk is low they pay up and take more of it, which makes the world riskier. When everyone says something is too risky at any price, it often becomes safe.
- **Parts:** how risk averse the crowd is; how much risk premium prices offer; what "everyone knows".
- **When:** when a trade feels safe and popular, or when an asset is shunned.
- **Score (guidance):** Popular and "safe" gets marked down; shunned and "too risky" gets a closer look.
- **Quote:** "I always say the riskiest thing in the world is the belief that there's no risk." (S015)
- **Sources:** 11 for the line (S015, S050, S051, S054, S057, S058, S060, S061, S063, S068); 12 on the perversity of risk (S084, S124, S126).

## 8. Three stages of a bull market (and of a bear market)
- **What:** Bull: first a few forward-looking people believe things will improve; second most see improvement; third everyone thinks things will improve forever. Bear: first a few see things are not rosy; second most see deterioration; third everyone agrees things can only get worse.
- **Parts:** which stage; how many believers versus skeptics.
- **When:** judging whether a decline is early or late.
- **Score (guidance):** First stage of a bull (third of a bear) is where bargains are. Third stage of a bull is where you get defensive.
- **Quote:** "Buying during the first stage can be highly profitable, while buying during the last will carry you over the cliff with the rest of the herd." (S077)
- **Sources:** 17 (S004, S015, S021, S030, S040, S049, S058, S059, S062, S073, S074, S077).

## 9. Second-level thinking
- **What:** First-level thinking says good company, so buy. Second-level thinking asks what is priced in, what others think, and what others think others think. You must be different and better than the consensus.
- **Parts:** the obvious view; the consensus already in the price; your differing view; why you are right.
- **When:** any idea that sounds obvious.
- **Score (guidance):** If your reason to act is the same reason everyone has, expect average results (S088).
- **Quote:** "How is it priced given: its current condition; how I think its condition will change; how others think it will change; and how others think others think it will change?" (S050)
- **Sources:** 9 by name (S011, S021, S028, S037, S039, S050, S129).

## 10. Avoid the losers (always good, sometimes great, never terrible)
- **What:** Oaktree's route to performance: excluding bad investments matters more than finding stars. Long-run results come from an absence of disasters.
- **Parts:** downside first; defense over offense; consistency over top-decile years.
- **When:** choosing between a bold strategy and a steady one.
- **Score (guidance):** Prefer the option whose worst case is survivable and whose results are steady, unless the upside is large and asymmetric.
- **Quote:** "If we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves." (S100)
- **Sources:** 13 (S009, S028, S058, S060, S084, S099, S100, S105, S111, S136).

## 11. Asymmetry
- **What:** Superior investing means more upside than downside: participating in gains more than in losses.
- **Parts:** upside if right; downside if wrong; how often you can find the gap.
- **When:** comparing any two bets.
- **Score (guidance):** A bet with equal upside and downside is not an edge even if likely to win.
- **Quote:** "The goal in investing is asymmetry: to expose yourself to return in a way that doesn't expose you commensurately to risk" (S055)
- **Sources:** 10 (S009, S028, S037, S045, S051, S054, S055, S077).

## 12. Bet sizing by odds (You Bet!)
- **What:** Investing is betting on propositions. The skill is choosing which outcome to back, how much, and when, given the odds offered. A favorite at a bad price is a bad bet.
- **Parts:** probability you are right; payout if right; payout if wrong; how much the crowd already backs the same horse.
- **When:** deciding position size.
- **Score (guidance):** Size up only when probability and payout both favor you; information everyone has does not count as an edge.
- **Quote:** "The size of your bet should take into account both the probability you are correct about who's going to win and the asymmetry of the potential payout." (S037)
- **Sources:** 4 (S037, S130, S014, S001).

## 13. Buy in tranches; never wait for the bottom
- **What:** The bottom can only be known afterward. Decide how much you want invested by the time the bottom is reached and buy part now, keeping money and nerve to buy more.
- **Parts:** target allocation; first tranche; reserve; a thesis check before each add.
- **When:** any sell-off.
- **Score (guidance):** All-in on day one and all-out waiting for clarity both fail. Buying some while prices fall, with reserves, passes.
- **Quote:** "What I would do is figure out how much you'll want to have invested by the time the bottom is reached - whenever that is - and spend part of it today." (S035)
- **Sources:** 13 (S021, S035, S036, S068, S073, S080, S100, S129).

## 14. INVESTCON levels
- **What:** A six-step scale for getting defensive when valuations are high: 6 stop buying; 5 reduce aggressive holdings and add defensive ones; 4 sell remaining aggressive holdings; 3 trim defensive holdings; 2 eliminate all holdings; 1 go short. He says he has never gone past the middle.
- **Parts:** valuation; investor behavior; the level that fits.
- **When:** markets look expensive and people ask whether to get out.
- **Score (guidance):** Levels 3, 2, and 1 need certainty no one has. In August 2025 he said INVESTCON 5 (S022).
- **Quote:** "I consider tactical actions in terms of the spectrum that runs from aggressiveness to defensiveness, and when valuations are high, I consider becoming more defensive." (S022)
- **Sources:** 1 by name (S022); the aggressive/defensive spectrum appears in 40.

## 15. Survive the low points (the six-foot man)
- **What:** Never forget the six-foot-tall man who drowned crossing a stream five feet deep on average. Averages do not matter if one bad moment ends you.
- **Parts:** worst plausible moment; leverage; liquidity; who can force you out.
- **When:** leverage, concentration, or any plan that only works on average.
- **Score (guidance):** Fails if a normal crisis forces a sale at the bottom.
- **Quote:** "It's not enough to be able to get through on average; you have to be able to survive life's low points." (S086)
- **Sources:** 13 (S008, S025, S037, S051, S054, S072, S080, S086, S098).

## 16. Dare to look wrong (being early)
- **What:** Superior results need non-consensus views that turn out right, and those positions look wrong for a while. Being too far ahead of your time is indistinguishable from being wrong.
- **Parts:** how different is the view; how long can you hold it; can you survive looking wrong.
- **When:** a contrarian position is losing money.
- **Score (guidance):** Before buying amid fear, plan for further declines and for looking foolish.
- **Quote:** "This is really the bottom-line: not whether you dare to be different or to be wrong, but whether you dare to look wrong." (S055)
- **Sources:** 15 (S011, S015, S044, S055, S060, S068, S073, S088).

## 17. Value as a magnet on price
- **What:** Value pulls price over time, but only over time. Price can run past value for years, which is how bubbles and crashes form.
- **Parts:** the gap; the time horizon; whether you can wait.
- **When:** an undervalued holding keeps falling, or an overvalued one keeps rising.
- **Score (guidance):** Cheapness is a reason to own, not a timing signal.
- **Quote:** "while the current relationship of price to underlying value should move in the expected direction, it can only be counted on to do so in the long run at best." (S022)
- **Sources:** 2 by name (S022, S138); the idea runs through S021 and S013.
