# Principles: Howard Marks

Built from 134 public sources (Oaktree memos 1990 to 2026, interviews and talks); see sources.md. Each rule: one plain sentence, verbatim quotes with source ids, number of supporting sources found in the corpus, and confidence (high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1).

1. **What you pay decides the outcome, not what you buy.**
   "It's not what you buy, it's what you pay that counts." (S015) "There is no security that is so good that it can't be overpriced, or so bad that it can't be underpriced." (S125)
   Sources: 18. Confidence: high.

2. **Never wait for the bottom; buy when value is cheap.**
   "Oaktree explicitly rejects the notion of waiting for the bottom; we buy when we can access value cheap." (S021) "I took the position that it would be a mistake to wait for an ascertainable bottom before doing so." (S035)
   Sources: 7. Confidence: high.

3. **The best bargains appear when people are most afraid.**
   "But it is when the knives are falling that the people are most terrified that the best bargains are available." (S129) "But, when unfavorable developments are raining down, that's often the best time to step up." (S014)
   Sources: 7 on the falling knife (S014, S035, S079, S129); 37 on contrarianism. Confidence: high.

4. **Do not let the falling-knife saw think for you.**
   "Like other adages, it can be entirely appropriate in some instances, while in others it's nothing but an excuse for failing to think independently." (S079)
   Sources: 5 (S014, S035, S079, S129, S021). Confidence: high.

5. **Buy some, keep reserves, and buy more if the thesis holds.**
   "If it gets cheaper and we conclude the thesis is still intact, we buy more." (S021) "Or they may continue down, in which case you'll have money left (and hopefully the nerve) to buy more." (S035)
   Sources: 13. Confidence: high.

6. **Know where you are even though you can't know where you're going.**
   "It's one of my first principles that we never know where we're going - given the unreliability of macro forecasting - but we ought to know where we are." (S057)
   Sources: 18. Confidence: high.

7. **Act only at extremes; in the middle, do little.**
   "Once in a while - once or twice a decade, perhaps - markets go so high or so low that the argument for action is compelling and the probability of being right is high." (S021) "So, one key is to avoid making macro calls too often." (S021)
   Sources: 3 (S021, S022, S136). Confidence: high.

8. **Psychology, not data, tells you where the cycle is.**
   "The discipline which is most important in investing is not accounting or economics, but psychology." (S125) "The key lies in understanding prevailing investor psychology." (S021)
   Sources: 30+ (pendulum alone 27). Confidence: high.

9. **No formula or rule works for long; judgment does.**
   "Superior investing always comes down to skillful analysis and superior insight, not adherence to formulas and guidelines." (S013) "a company's attributes can't be summed up through an algorithm or reduced to a single number." (S022)
   Sources: 5 (S013, S020, S021, S022, S037). Confidence: high.

10. **Information everyone has is not an edge.**
    "But one of the most important things to know about gambling is that information that's available to everyone isn't likely to produce winnings." (S037) "Since other investors may be smart, well informed and highly computerized, you must find an edge they don't have." (S011)
    Sources: 6 (S007, S011, S020, S021, S037, S050). Confidence: high.

11. **Risk is permanent loss; ride out volatility.**
    "We can ride out volatility, but we never get a chance to undo a permanent loss." (S054) "I define risk as the probability of a bad outcome, and volatility is, at best, an indicator of the presence of risk." (S028)
    Sources: 12. Confidence: high.

12. **The riskiest thing is overpaying; the safest is a low price.**
    "At bottom, the riskiest thing is overpaying for an asset (regardless of its quality), and the best way to reduce risk is by paying a price that's irrationally low (ditto)." (S054)
    Sources: 4 (S051, S054, S086, S100). Confidence: high.

13. **When everyone thinks there's no risk, risk is highest.**
    "I always say the riskiest thing in the world is the belief that there's no risk." (S015)
    Sources: 11. Confidence: high.

14. **What everyone knows is usually wrong, and already in the price.**
    "What's clear to the broad consensus of investors is almost always wrong." (S084) "Before acting on a forecast, we must ask whether there's good reason to think we're more right than the consensus view already embodied in prices." (S105)
    Sources: 6 (S062, S084, S105, S125, S088, S109). Confidence: high.

15. **But be contrarian only at extremes; usually the consensus is about right.**
    "most of the time, the consensus is as close to right as most individuals can get." (S021)
    Sources: 2 (S021, S109). Confidence: medium.

16. **Avoid the losers and the winners take care of themselves.**
    "If we avoid the losers, the winners will take care of themselves." (S100) "the best foundation for above-average long-term performance is an absence of disasters." (S009)
    Sources: 13. Confidence: high.

17. **Survive the low points; averages do not save you.**
    "It's not enough to be able to get through on average; you have to be able to survive life's low points." (S086)
    Sources: 13. Confidence: high.

18. **Leverage only on assets that cannot fall permanently.**
    "The more your assets are prone to permanent loss, the less leverage you should employ." (S072)
    Sources: 4 (S071, S072, S007, S086). Confidence: high.

19. **Not acting is also a decision.**
    "The decision to not act - to leave a portfolio unchanged - should be scrutinized as critically as a decision to make changes." (S014)
    Sources: 2 (S014, S039). Confidence: medium.

20. **Dare to look wrong for a while.**
    "And one other thing: you have to be willing to look wrong for a while." (S060) "In addition to superior skill, successful investing requires the ability to look wrong for a while and survive some mistakes." (S011)
    Sources: 15. Confidence: high.

21. **Hold value views with conviction, but conviction in a wrong view is lethal.**
    "If there's no conviction, a drop in the price of a holding can weaken the investor's faith in the estimate and make him fail to buy more" (S100) "Few things are more dangerous than an incorrect opinion held with conviction and relied on to excess." (S100)
    Sources: 3 (S071, S100, S105). Confidence: high.

22. **A low price on horrible fundamentals is not a bargain.**
    "You don't want to buy something which has horrible fundamentals at a really low seeming price." (S138)
    Sources: 1 verbatim; inferred from S073 and S021 (they bought senior debt of good companies). Confidence: low.

23. **Separate price drops caused by psychology from drops caused by the business.**
    "few of the recent sharp price declines were associated with" ... "weakness in the depreciating assets or the companies behind them." (S073)
    Sources: 3 (S013, S049, S073). Confidence: high.

24. **Every forecast needs a probability attached.**
    "In addition to a forecast, you also need a good sense for the probability your forecast is correct, since not all forecasts are created equal." (S014)
    Sources: 3 (S001, S014, S073). Confidence: high.

25. **Sell for reasons about the investment, never about your nerves.**
    "these reasons should be based on the outlook for the investment - not the psyche of the investor" (S018) "Selling an asset is a decision that must not be considered in isolation." (S018)
    Sources: 2 (S018, S020). Confidence: medium.

26. **Get defensive by degrees when prices are high; never all the way.**
    "it's essentially impossible to reasonably reach the degree of certainty needed to implement INVESTCON 3, 2, or 1." (S022)
    Sources: 1 by name; defensive tilts in 40. Confidence: medium.
