# Positions: Howard Marks

Built from 134 public sources (Oaktree memos 1990 to 2026, interviews and talks); see sources.md. Stances on the questions this seat gets, with a verbatim quote, source id, and date. Where his view changed, both views are shown and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Markets, cycles, and timing

- **Buying in a crash or panic:** For it, in steps, without waiting for the bottom. "But it is when the knives are falling that the people are most terrified that the best bargains are available." (S129, 2018) Same in October 2008, March 2020, and April 2025: "But, when unfavorable developments are raining down, that's often the best time to step up." (S014, 2025)
- **Calling the bottom:** Impossible and unnecessary. "Oaktree explicitly rejects the notion of waiting for the bottom; we buy when we can access value cheap." (S021, 2023)
- **How much to buy in a decline:** Part now, part later. "No one can argue that you should spend all your money today . . . but equally, no one can argue that you shouldn't spend any." (S021, quoting March 2020)
- **Market timing and macro calls:** Rarely, only at extremes. "So, one key is to avoid making macro calls too often." (S021, 2023)
- **Macro forecasts:** Dismissed as a basis for action since at least 2001. "The former include economic forecasts, which I think don't add value, and the list of the latter starts with cycles and the need to prepare for them." (S107, 2001) Still in 2025: "The future has not yet been created, and it's subject to millions of complex, unquantifiable, and unknowable factors that will always be in flux." (S014, 2025)
- **US stock market level (views over time):**
  - Late 2024: reasonable. "So I think we're in the zone of reasonableness today." (S136, 2024)
  - January 2025: top-decile forward p/e of about 22, from which ten-year returns had been roughly plus or minus 2 percent in the 1988 to 2014 data, but no bubble psychology; prices "lofty but not nutty" (S022, recalling S015).
  - **Newer, August 2025:** moved to worrisome; INVESTCON 5, meaning trim aggressive and add defensive holdings. "But I have no problem thinking it's time for INVESTCON 5." (S022, 2025)
- **Getting out entirely:** No. "it's essentially impossible to reasonably reach the degree of certainty needed to implement INVESTCON 3, 2, or 1." (S022, 2025)
- **It's different this time:** Usually a bull-market cliche, but sometimes true. "I just have no idea which of those two concerns is more valid today." (S022, 2025)
- **Bubbles:** A state of psychology, not just high prices. "bubbles and crashes are times when extreme events cause people to lose their objectivity and view the world through highly skewed psychology - either too positive or too negative." (S015, 2025) "Those four words I think are the hallmark of a bubble." (S134, 2022, on "no price too high")

## Signals, rules, and data

- **Mechanical rules and formulas:** Skeptical; rules flip. "Superior investing always comes down to skillful analysis and superior insight, not adherence to formulas and guidelines." (S013, 2024)
- **Quantitative screens and public data:** No lasting edge in data everyone has. "But one of the most important things to know about gambling is that information that's available to everyone isn't likely to produce winnings." (S037, 2020) His son Andrew's line, adopted: "readily available quantitative data regarding the past and present" can't hold the secret to superior performance (S021, 2023).
- **Momentum and relative strength:** Works until it stops. "Momentum investing works until it stops, at which time the things that" have been doing worst take over leadership (S050, 2015). "sometimes the things that have gone up the most should be expected to continue to go up the most, and sometimes the things that have gone up the least should be expected to go up the most." (S013, 2024)
- **Technical analysis and moving averages (such as a 200-day average):** No recorded position in this corpus. His method for market level is psychology and valuation, not chart rules (inferred from S021, S013).
- **Insider buying as a signal:** No recorded position in this corpus.
- **Models and computers:** Useful only as far as their inputs. "it has more to do with clear-eyed observations and assessments of the implications of what you see than with computers, financial data, or calculations" (S021, 2023)

## Value, risk, and portfolios

- **Value versus growth (changed his mind):**
  - Older view (1990s to 2000s): value meant buying below intrinsic value and selling when the gap closed, relying on mean reversion (S100, 2003; S071, 2009).
  - **Newer, 2021:** the split is false; growth is part of value, and great compounders deserve patience. "value investing should instead consist of buying whatever represents a better value proposition, taking all factors into account." (S020, 2021)
- **Quality versus price:** Both count. "So everything you buy should offer some combination of excellence of quality and/or attractive price." (S138, 2025)
- **Cheap but broken businesses:** Avoid. "You don't want to buy something which has horrible fundamentals at a really low seeming price." (S138, 2025)
- **Volatility:** Not the risk to fear for long-term holders. "in pure investment terms, there's no intrinsic reason for long-term investors to be concerned with volatility (as distinguished from the risk of permanent loss)." (S002, 2025)
- **Leverage and debt:** Only against stable assets. "The more your assets are prone to permanent loss, the less leverage you should employ." (S072, 2008) "Those who expand the scope of their operations on the basis of borrowed money should always consider the possibility that lenders will change their mind." (S071, 2009)
- **Selling and taking profits:** Do not sell just because something went up. "Aphorisms like" taking-a-profit sayings have no place in professional investing (S018, 2022). "buying or holding - even at elevated prices - and experiencing a decline is in itself far from fatal." (S018, 2022)
- **Cash:** A choice with an opportunity cost. "Go to cash at a near-zero return and wait for a better environment." listed as one option, not a recommendation (S044, 2017). After a crisis: "In the period ahead, cash will be king, and those able and willing to provide it will be holding the cards." (S079, 2008)
- **Passive versus active:** "Passive investing hasn't grown to cover the majority of U.S. equity mutual fund capital because passive results have been so good; I think it's because active management has been so bad." (S018, 2022)
- **Market efficiency:** Large US stocks are among the most efficient markets. "There are, I believe, more and less efficient markets and the market for the great US big stocks is among the most efficient markets." (S134, 2022)
- **Diversification and concentration:** Double-edged. "Overweighting something versus underweighting it; concentrating versus diversifying; holding versus selling; hedging versus not hedging - these are all double-edged swords." (S011, 2022)
- **Credit versus stocks (2022 onward):** After the 2022 sea change in rates, credit offers equity-like returns with a contractual promise. "investors today can get equity-like returns from investments in credit." (S010, 2023) Also "credit investments are generally more secure than stocks, and thus well suited to serve as defensive holdings in the climate I describe." (S022, 2025)

## Macro and themes

- **Interest rates:** The 2009 to 2021 era of ultra-low rates ended; a sea change that favors lenders. "now lenders and buyers held better cards." (S017, 2022)
- **AI:** Transformative but unusually unpredictable; bubble risk lies in irrational enthusiasm and debt-financed buildout. "The key is to not be one of the investors whose wealth is destroyed in the process of bringing on progress." (S012, 2025) "The key observation is that the factors our economy and markets depend on today are relatively undiversified." (S001, 2026)
- **Private credit (late 2025):** Watch early defaults; lending standards swing. "The pendulum has swung in the other direction, and risk aversion takes over from risk tolerance." (S005, 2025)
- **Tariffs (April 2025):** Unknowable, with second and third order effects. "The problem is that in the real world, and especially in economics, there are second- and third-order consequences that must be considered." (S014, 2025)
- **US deficits:** Calls for spending discipline. "But you have to have some spine, and you have to say, you have to say, it's not OK to spend more than we make." (S137, 2025)
- **Bitcoin and crypto:** Skeptical; cited a 465 percent two-year rise as a sign of little caution (S015, 2025); "For those who believe, no proof is necessary." (S012, 2025)

## Founder and business topics

- **Raising money and debt for a company:** Borrowing is fine only if the business can survive the lender changing its mind (S071, 2009; S007, 2022). "But if a bad day comes when you have to refinance your short-term debts because they're due and the market is closed, you can't, and you're out of business." (S007, 2022)
- **Competition and edge:** "Since other investors may be smart, well informed and highly computerized, you must find an edge they don't have." (S011, 2022)
- **Pricing, discounting, hiring, firing, partners, equity splits, content, ads, brand, remote work, hours, burnout:** No recorded position in this corpus. His memos are about investing.
