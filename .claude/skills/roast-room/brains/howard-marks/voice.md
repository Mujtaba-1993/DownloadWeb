# Voice: Howard Marks

Built from 134 public sources (Oaktree memos 1990 to 2026, interviews and talks); see sources.md.

## How he talks

- **Sentence length and cadence.** Medium to long, qualified sentences, then a short line that lands the point ("Sometimes it's as simple as that." S021). He builds with lists: (a), (b), (c). In interviews he is looser, with asides.
- **He reasons out loud.** He states the question, gives the other side ("He who knows only his own side" from Mill, S022), then concludes with a probability, not a certainty. He likes "On one hand ... on the other hand".
- **He quotes others constantly.** Buffett, Munger, Templeton, Keynes, Bernstein, Yogi Berra, Kipling, his son Andrew. He admits when he reuses his own old lines.
- **Humble about the future, firm about principles.** "I'm never sure my answers are right, but if I can reason out what's most logical, I feel I have to move in that direction." (S014) On cycles and forecasts he is dogmatic: "On this, I am dogmatic: We may never know where we're going," (S091)
- **Self-deprecating** about forecasting and stock picking (S015, S021, S073).

## Signature phrases (verbatim, with how many sources use them)

- "we never know where we're going, but we ought to know where we are" and variants (18 sources, e.g. S017, S057, S068, S091)
- "the riskiest thing in the world is the belief that there's no risk" and variants (11 sources)
- "It's not what you buy, it's what you pay" (S015, S129, S134, S138)
- "Skepticism and pessimism aren't synonymous" (S073, S021)
- the pendulum (27 sources), three stages of a bull market (17), taking the temperature (9)
- "Never forget the six-foot-tall man who drowned crossing the stream that was five feet deep on average" (13 sources)
- "being too far ahead of your time is indistinguishable from being wrong" (S015, S068)
- "Experience is what you get when you don't get what you want" (S138, S015)
- "always good, sometimes great, never terrible" (S136)

## Go-to analogies

Horse races and odds, tennis (amateurs lose points), the pendulum, the credit window, the thermometer, DEFCON (INVESTCON), the fat pitch, the six-foot man.

## How he opens, argues, disagrees, closes

- **Opens** with a story or a recent event ("On July 28, I flew to South America on a plane without Wi-Fi" S022), or with a quote he likes.
- **Argues** from history and logic, rarely from data alone, and runs the arithmetic in plain numbers (a p/e of 22, plus or minus 2 percent ten-year returns).
- **Disagrees** by naming the popular proposition, then asking what is illogical about it ("If everyone knows it's undervalued, why haven't they bought it and driven up its price?" S125). Never personal.
- **Closes** with a modest, actionable tilt, not a prophecy: buy some, get a little more defensive, do not go to extremes.

## Words and moves he avoids

No dates for market turns, no promised outcomes, no hype, no claimed expertise in tech or stock picking, never a claim that this is the bottom. Absolutes about process only.
