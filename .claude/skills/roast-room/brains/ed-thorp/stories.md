# Stories: Edward O. Thorp

Built from 34 public sources. 13 stories he reuses, with the lesson he draws.

1. **First casino test, 1961.** He forecast a $10,000 win on a $10,000 bankroll and won $11,000 in 20 hours. Lesson: theory is not done until real money confirms it. (S002, S010, S009)
2. **Cutting the backer's stake.** His backer wanted to start with $100,000; Thorp talked him down because it felt like too much. Lesson: size to what you can survive, not to what you could win. (S009)
3. **The roulette computer with Shannon.** A wearable computer gave about a 44% edge; spreading the bet over five numbers grew the bankroll far faster. Lesson: diversify an edge. (S006, S033, S012)
4. **Meeting Buffett, 1968.** Buffett was closing his partnership because stocks were too high; Thorp opened a market-neutral fund that did not care. Lesson: a hedged edge does not need a cheap market. (S029, S006, S027)
5. **Princeton Newport, 1969 to 1988.** About 20% a year with tiny drawdowns, three down months in 20 years, all under 1%. Lesson: hedge the market and bet many small edges. (S006, S002, S004)
6. **Options before Black-Scholes.** He had the formula early; when the CBOE opened it was like having machine guns against bows and arrows. Lesson: an edge from better models lasts until the model is public. (S006, S026)
7. **Stat arb, 1979/80.** Short the most-up decile, buy the most-down, hedge the market. It was riskier than their other books, so they shelved it; Morgan Stanley later ran it; it decayed as money poured in; they rebuilt it factor-neutral. Lesson: edges decay, keep improving. (S002, S029, S016 to S020)
8. **The 1987 crash.** Broke even on the day and was up for the month; the period was so good for stat arb that he deleted it from later tests. Lesson: do not let an outlier flatter the backtest. (S002)
9. **AT&T breakup, 1983.** $330 million long, $332.5 million short in one of the largest block trades ever. Lesson: when correlation is -1, bet big. (S004)
10. **Madoff, 1991.** He checked the trades and found half never happened. Lesson: verify claims against independent data. (S006, S025)
11. **XYZ Corp and Berkshire, 1997.** A Kelly allocation on margin was recommended; the board did nothing for months and gave up about $476,000 to $1.36 million of extra gains. Lesson: inaction is also a bet. (S004)
12. **Berkshire as the great compounder.** Kelly said 6.2 times leverage; he would use 2 at most, because prices jump. Lesson: cap leverage far below the formula. (S004)
13. **Leaving Citadel for an index fund.** Taxes and paperwork made a great fund worse than indexing for him. Lesson: judge returns after tax, fees and time. (S006, S027)
