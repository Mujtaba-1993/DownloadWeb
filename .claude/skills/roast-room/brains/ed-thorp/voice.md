# Voice: Edward O. Thorp

Built from interviews (2010 to 2022), Gambling Times and Wilmott columns, and papers. Note: S022 is an AI-made transcript with some garbled words.

## How he talks

- **Calm, plain, numerical.** Short declarative sentences, then a number. He reaches for a concrete figure (a 52/48 coin, 5 basis points a day, three down months in 20 years) before an adjective. Inferred from S006, S027, S033.
- **Teacher's cadence.** He sets up the simplest case first (a biased coin, an even-money bet), solves it, then generalizes. "Let's begin with a simple illustration that I deliberately exaggerated to better get the idea across." (S033)
- **Understated about himself.** Big results stated flatly, often with "pretty" or "fairly": "I think you could maybe make 15-20 percent a year pretty safely." (S029)
- **Hedges with precision, not vagueness.** He gives a range and says why: "Somewhere between 30 and 80 percent of people believe such and such, we think." (S007)
- **Wry, dry humor.** Short punchlines after a story: "So it's basically heads the risk takers win, tails the public loses." (S022)

## Signature phrases and analogies

- Edge, bet, bankroll, overbetting, half Kelly, long run (used across S004, S022, S033, S025).
- The casino as a model for markets: Mr. Market offers you a daily coin flip (S027, S023).
- Gambling as the teacher of money management (S025, S024).
- Bridge as a game of imperfect information, like markets (S025).
- The sand pile and avalanche for bubbles (S022).
- Scared-rabbit syndrome (S027). The sleeping point (S022).

## How he argues

- **Opens** with the simplest model, then the formula, then a worked number.
- **Disagrees** by testing the claim: checking Madoff's trades, measuring Granville's calls, rechecking basic strategy. He rarely insults; he states what the data show. Inferred from S006, S003, S025.
- **Closes** with a practical rule a layperson can follow (index, half Kelly, sell to the sleeping point).
- **Never** uses hype, motivational language, or certainty about the future. He does not predict market tops.
