# Positions: Edward O. Thorp

Built from 34 public sources, 1961 to 2022. Weighted to the seat's pillar: measured edge and bet size. Newer views marked where they changed. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Edge and sizing (the pillar)

- **What counts as an edge (2018 to 2022):** excess return after risk and costs, demonstrable with a reason. "By “edge,” I mean excess return after adjusting for risk, net of costs." (S025, compiled 2022)
- **Sizing (1962 onward, unchanged):** Kelly sizing, then half of it. "So I generally recommend that people just bet half-kelly, so they won't get scared." (S022, 2017)
- **Uncertain edge (2006):** assume the real edge is below the estimate and cut the bet. "uncertain, long term compounders should further limit their investment fraction enough" (S004, 2006)
- **Backtests and data mining (2006):** systems that worked may be data mined; expect live results below tests. "may be partly or entirely based on data mining so mt may be substantially less" (S004, 2006)
- **Outliers in a test (2003):** delete periods that are too good to recur. "It was so good that in future tests and simulations we" (S002, 2003)
- **Tails and worst case (2003):** history understates extremes; the lognormal model understates big moves. "previous historical limits on financial" (S002, 2003)
- **Leverage (2006, 2017):** cap far below Kelly; leverage is behind the big disasters. "Most of them come from too much leverage and from the framework of the Kelly criteria, it means you're betting too much." (S022, 2017)
- **Costs (2003):** first order; can halve a gross edge. "Commissions and market impact costs reduced this to about 26%." (S002, 2003)
- **Edge decay (2003, 2017):** edges fade as money finds them. "Then it began to lose some of its power as Morgan Stanley and others spread the idea and also put more money into it." (S029, 2017)
- **Edges anyone has heard of (2022):** "I think the first fundamental thing is, if everybody’s heard about it already, there’s, historically at least, no reason to believe that you have any kind of edge." (S007, 2022)
- **Short-term reversal signal (2003, 2017):** a real, documented anomaly for him (long the most-down decile, short the most-up), but riskier than his other books and it decayed. Inferred from S002, S029.
- **Market timing:** no recorded position of his own beyond doubt that bubbles can be timed. "So as far as asset classes now, it's always hard to know when you're in a bubble." (S022, 2017)
- **How to judge a track record (2011 compilation):** pick the closest index and compare; with many managers, the best records are partly luck. "I would say that the burden of proof is on the non-index manager." (S025)
- **Diversification (1979):** splitting an edge across bets raises growth. "Such is the power of diversification." (S033, 1979)
- **Stop rules and drawdowns:** no recorded stop-loss rule. He measures drawdown risk through bet size (S001, 2010: probability of loss falls from 12.4% at full Kelly to 3.5% at half Kelly in one simulation). Paraphrase.
- **Sell to a level you can live with (2017):** "So the advice he gave was sell down to the sleeping point. I like that." (S022, 2017)

## Investing generally

- **Index funds (2017 to 2022):** default for nearly everyone. "People should be putting money into index funds when they can’t demonstrate that an investment with similar characteristics is better." (S025)
- **Efficient markets (2003 to 2022):** not true in detail, still the right default. "So, I don’t think EMH is quite the right mental framework for thinking about markets, but it’s a good start for almost everybody." (S025)
- **Active management and fees (2011):** "Actually, what seems to happen, from what I can see, is that the underperformance roughly matches the fees." (S025)
- **Hedge funds (2022):** a few good ones, but too expensive and tax-inefficient for taxable investors. "There are a few good hedge funds around, but they take too much for the general partner and leave too little for the limited partner." (S006, 2022)
- **Long-term holding (2022):** "The answer is, if you’re a long-term investor, you should just buy and hold equities." (S006, 2022)
- **His own portfolio (2018):** Berkshire as a value index, hedge funds for spending money. "It’s my single investment in the stock market." (S027, 2018). Changed by 2022: he exited Citadel for an index fund over taxes (S006, 2022). Newer view: simplicity after tax wins.
- **Market level (2018):** "Stocks today are on the high side and will be hurt as rates rise." (S027, 2018)
- **Behavior (2018):** the scared-rabbit syndrome of selling bottoms and buying tops. (S027)
- **Fads, crypto, meme stocks (2022):** crowd madness. "It might be good reading for people who are involved in cryptocurrency now, for instance. Or in GameStop." (S007, 2022)
- **Fraud and due diligence (1991 story, told 2022):** check trades against exchange data. "So I looked at all their trades and I saw that half the trades never happened when I researched them." (S006, 2022)
- **Quant funds versus judgment (2018):** data misses things like smart buybacks. "The quant funds trawl through massive amounts of data." (S027, 2018)
- **Beating the market as a career (2017):** only if you give your life to it. "But if you just want to make lesser amounts of money, I don't know what to tell a person at this point because you can do so well knowing nothing in the market." (S029, 2017)
- **Bank leverage and bailouts (2017):** "So it's basically heads the risk takers win, tails the public loses." (S022, 2017)
- **Statistics literacy (2022):** absolute versus relative risk, averages versus distributions, fake precision in polls. (S007)
- **Capital and independence (2022):** "If you have enough capital, it will support you indefinitely." (S006, 2022)
- **Time versus money (2018):** "At 85, the marginal value of time is higher and the marginal value of money is lower." (S027, 2018)
- **Real versus nominal (1980):** "What counts is real buying power, not nominal dollars." (S034, 1980)
