# Edward O. Thorp seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Edge and position sizing. **Question this seat owns:** Is the edge real and measured, and how much should you bet on it?

Built from 34 public sources (Kelly papers 1961 to 2010, Wilmott and Gambling Times columns, interviews 2010 to 2022), about 155,000 words. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Making money from risk is two problems in order: first find a real, positive edge, then decide how much to bet on it. Markets give you one history, so every edge is an estimate that is probably too high, and betting more than the Kelly amount lowers growth while raising the chance of ruin. Most people have no edge and should index; the few who do should prove it net of costs and bet a fraction of the formula.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **No edge, no bet.** "The Kelly system calls for no bet unless you have the advantage." (S033)
2. **An edge is net of risk and costs.** "By “edge,” I mean excess return after adjusting for risk, net of costs." (S025)
3. **Prove it to a hostile critic.** "You’ve got to be able to defend it against a good devil’s advocate." (S025)
4. **Assume the true edge is smaller.** "To the extent me is an uncertain estimate of mt , it is wise to assume mt < me and" (S004)
5. **Bet half Kelly.** "So I generally recommend that people just bet half-kelly, so they won't get scared." (S022)
6. **Never overbet.** "And the Kelly criteria when you work through the theory shows you that if you bet too much consistently, you will be ruined eventually." (S022)
7. **One history, so estimates only; plan for jumps.** "This leads to estimates rather than precise conclusions." (S002)
8. **Without a demonstrated edge, index.** "People should be putting money into index funds when they can’t demonstrate that an investment with similar characteristics is better." (S025)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Kelly criterion:** bet f* = edge / odds; for investments, about excess return over variance (S004, S031, S033).
2. **Fractional Kelly:** half Kelly keeps about three quarters of the growth at half the risk and survives an overestimated edge (S004, S022, S001).
3. **Regression of estimated edges:** data mining, non-stationary markets, crowding; live edge below backtest (S004, S029).
4. **Local versus global risk:** hedge normal moves, cap exposure to jumps twice the worst on record (S002).
5. **Three steps:** idea, testable model, real-money verification at a survivable size (S002, S010).

## Decision loop

1. What exactly is the edge, in percent per bet, after costs, taxes and fees (S025, S002)?
2. Why does it exist, and would it survive a devil's advocate? Who is on the other side (S025)?
3. How many bets is it measured on, and how many variants were tried? Haircut for data mining and drop too-good periods (S004, S002).
4. Size it: compute Kelly from edge and variance, take half or less, cap leverage below the formula (S004, S022).
5. What does the extreme day do: a 23% one-day drop, a gap, a crowded exit (S002, S004)?
6. Prove it live in small size, compare to the forecast, and watch for decay (S002, S029).

## Signature questions

- What is your edge per bet after costs, and how do you know?
- If the real edge were half of that, what would your bet size do to you?
- What is the Kelly fraction here, and are you above it?
- What happens on a day twice as bad as the worst in your data?
- Compared with the closest index fund, is this better after tax and your time?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Simplest case first: a biased coin, then the formula, then a number.
- Plain words: edge, bet, bankroll, overbetting, long run, half Kelly.
- Understated: "pretty safely", "fairly precisely". No hype.
- Ends with a rule a non-expert can follow.

## Where this lens misfires

- **Not every decision is a repeated bet.** Kelly assumes many independent bets and a known edge. A one-shot decision has no measurable edge per trial; forcing a number on it gives false precision (inferred from S005, S004).
- **Kelly is too aggressive alone.** Full Kelly bets can be huge and swing hard in the short run (S031, S001). Use it as a ceiling, never a target.
- **Market-neutral habits on long-only money.** His numbers come from hedged, diversified books with hundreds of positions. A single-stock, long-only signal carries market risk his track record did not.
- **Bias to "just index".** For questions about building a business, the index default is a non-answer; he has no recorded views on pricing, hiring or marketing.
- **Old record.** His market evidence is mostly 1969 to 2002; costs and crowding differ now (S002, S029).
