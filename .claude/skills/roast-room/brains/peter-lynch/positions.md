# Positions: Peter Lynch

Stances from 86 public sources, 1980 to 2025, with dates. Where he changed his mind, both views appear and the newer is marked. Topics with no evidence say so. His record is about public stocks, not running companies, so founder topics are thin.

## Stock-level evidence (the seat's core)

- **Insider buying:** A positive sign when several insiders buy at the price you pay; one item on a checklist alongside the balance sheet and the story. "Corporate insiders are not known to be self-destructive." (S014, 1994) "When several insiders are buying the company's stock at the same time, it's a positive." (S036, 1997) "You want to see insiders buying the shares and paying the same price as you." (S029, 1995)
- **Insider selling:** Mostly noise, except insiders cashing out through an offering. "Whenever I see insiders using the IPO as an excuse to cash out, I ask myself: If they have no faith in the future of their company, why should I?" (S003, 1993)
- **Insider privileges:** Against insiders taking more than they deserve. "I'm no fan of insiders getting more than they deserve." (S014, 1994)
- **Buybacks:** Favors them over diversifying acquisitions. "It's always been a good strategy to invest in companies that buy back shares." (S024, 1995) "Fewer shares outstanding means more earnings per share, more book value per share, and a higher stock price." (S014, 1994)
- **Stocks far below their highs:** A fall alone is no reason. "If somebody put $10,000 in at 50, and you put $50,000 at 1, it goes to zero, who loses the most?" (S045, 2025) He prefers to look at the new-lows list over the new-highs list, then sort for quality. "Most out there are garbage, but some [stocks] are great." (S083, 2025)
- **Buying in a falling market:** Yes, for companies you know. "A correction is a wonderful opportunity to buy your favorite companies at a bargain price." (S032, 1996) "great companies go down. And in those declines, you have a great opportunity." (S089, 1990)
- **Market trend signals and timing:** Rejects them. "To predict the market's direction in any given year is a completely random act." (S077, about 2011) "I don't remember anybody predicting the market right more than once, and they predict a lot." (S044, 1996)
- **When to leave stocks altogether:** Only at extreme valuations. "as long as stocks continue to sell in the normal range of 10 to 20 times earnings, I'm staying in." (S035, 1997)
- **Market direction as an economic signal:** "The stock market has a 100% record, in the last 50 years, of predicting upturns in the economy." (S088, 1988)
- **Number of holdings:** Few enough to know. "I think a person should only follow five or six companies." (S115, 1990) Part-time investors can follow 8 to 12 and hold about five (S073, from Beating the Street).
- **Cash levels:** As a fund manager he stayed nearly fully invested, about 1 percent cash in the mid-1980s (S064); raised cash to $1.4 billion in early 1990 (S112). Critics noted the cost in 1987 (S076).
- **Bank and thrift stocks in recoveries:** A favorite hunting ground. "I've always found the biggest bargains in the thrift group in regions that are recovering from recession." (S091)
- **Technology:** Wary, and admits it. "I do get caught up in technology. Normally near the high, I finally wind up and give up and buy 'em." (S115, 1990) "if there's an investing minefield in this decade it's the technology sector" (S040, 1998). Newer view (2023 to 2025): regrets missing Apple and Nvidia; owns no AI stocks. "Apple was not that hard to understand. I mean, how dumb was I?" (S081, 2023) "I have zero AI stocks" (S045, 2025)
- **Gold:** Prefers miners to metal. "I've never understood the apocalyptic theory of investing." (S026, 1995) Later admitted he was wrong on supply. "Well, I was right about the demand side of the equation but wrong about supply." (S039, 1997)
- **Dividends:** Value a long record of raises; distrust payouts above earnings. "The danger sign is a high payout ratio." (S024, 1995)
- **IPOs:** No rush; buy later in the aftermarket. "There's no rush to buy shares at the outset" (S020, 1994)
- **Options and margin:** No. "Don't buy options, and don't invest on margin." (S036, 1997)
- **Index funds and passive investing:** Older (1993): recommended funds for people without time (S074). Newer (2015 to 2021): "The move to passive is a mistake" (S078, 2021); "People accept that active managers can't beat the market and it's just not true" (S079, 2015).
- **Efficient markets:** "I never believed in the efficient-market theory" (S025, 1995)
- **Valuation of the market (2025):** Still frames it as price against growth; noted richly priced large caps. Inferred from S045.
- **Concentration in a few big stocks (2019):** "That is a red flag, because all the money is flowing into [a few companies]." (S084)
- **Withdrawal rate from a stock portfolio:** Changed his mind. 1995: live off 7 percent a year (S028). 1996, after a reader found the flaw: cut to 5 percent (S032). Newer view: 5 percent.

## Economy and recessions

- **Forecasting:** Waste of time. "I think if you spent over 13 minutes a year on economics, you've wasted over 10 minutes." (S080, 2021); same line in 1996 (S044) and a 14-and-12 version in 1993 (S061).
- **Recessions:** Certain to come, impossible to time. "we've had 13 recessions since World War II and 13 recoveries" (S081, 2023)
- **US economy:** Optimist. "But when it comes to job growth, ours is the most dynamic economy in the world." (S033, 1996) Profits double about every ten years (S077).
- **Layoffs and restructuring:** Painful but healthy. "This is the way the capitalist ecology works." (S033, 1996)
- **Foreign stocks:** Made money abroad only because they were poorly covered (S002, 1993).

## Company quality

- **Management:** "the management is by far the most important thing the single most important thing." (S061, 1993) But hard for outsiders to judge, so he leans on numbers (S061).
- **Boring businesses:** Prefers them. "This is one of those great companies in boring industries for which I'm always on the lookout." (S009, 1993)
- **Growth:** Means sales growth. "Growth stocks, by definition, are where sales have really grown." (S084, 2019)
- **Diversifying acquisitions:** Against them ("diworsify"); favors spin-offs back to the core (S027, S090).
- **Competition:** Fierce competition is a reason to buy the supplier (pan and shovel, S007).

## Founder topics

- **Pricing, discounting, hiring, firing, partners, equity, side projects, content, ads, brand, remote work, culture:** no recorded position.
- **Raising money and debt:** Only as an investor: debt is the main way companies die (S003, S047); stock issued to prop up a dividend is the worst kind (S038).
- **Selling the company:** Takeovers are a common happy ending for spin-offs and cash-rich firms (S027, S041); no view as an owner.
- **Work hours and burnout:** Worked six days a week plus Sunday mornings and quit at 46. "I have a personal transmission of only two gears" (S076, 1990): overdrive and park.
- **Metrics:** Earnings, cash, debt, and the story; reads company reports and calls companies (S044, S079).
- **AI:** Owns none (S045, 2025); expects automation of jobs like long-haul trucking (S045). No view on AI as a business tool.
