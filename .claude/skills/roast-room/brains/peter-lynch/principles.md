# Principles: Peter Lynch

Decision rules from 86 public sources (Worth columns 1993 to 1999, talks and interviews 1980 to 2025). Each rule: one plain sentence, verbatim support with source ids, number of supporting sources, and confidence (high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1).

1. **Know what you own, or don't own it.** "If you don't understand it, it doesn't work. This is the single biggest principle." (S122) "I have this expression, know what you own." (S045) Sources: 13+. High.

2. **A falling price is never the thesis.** "The important thing is not the fact that the stock went from $3 to $6. Why did it go up? What happened to the story?" (S085) "'The sucker is going up' is not a good reason." (S081) Sources: 8 (S045, S047, S061, S081, S084, S085, S114, S122). High.

3. **Check the balance sheet before you buy.** "Before you invest, check the balance sheet to see if the company is financially sound." (S036) "if you can't understand the balance sheet, you probably shouldn't own it." (S079) Sources: 13+. High.

4. **Little or no debt is the best protection in a downturn.** "A company with no debt will have a hard time going bankrupt." (S003) "If the stock is falling but the company still makes sense and has no debt, stick by it," (S114) Sources: 7 (S003, S020, S046, S047, S061, S073, S114). High.

5. **Declines are certain and unpredictable, so prepare instead of predicting.** "So you take advantage of these declines. They're going to happen. No one knows when they're going to happen." (S047) "Corrections are unpredictable. By selling stocks to avoid pain, you can miss the next gain." (S032) Sources: 12. High.

6. **More money is lost anticipating corrections than in them.** "more people have lost money waiting for corrections and anticipating corrections than in the actual corrections." (S080) Sources: 5 (S028, S080, S083, S085, S118). High.

7. **Ignore economic and market forecasts; look at what is happening now.** "I think if you spent over 13 minutes a year on economics, you've wasted over 10 minutes." (S080) "The only thing I would look at is what's happening right now." (S080) Sources: 14 (S044, S048, S053, S061, S067, S077, S080, S081, S088, S112, S123). High.

8. **The stomach decides, not the brain.** "In the stock market, the most important organ is the stomach. It's not the brain." (S080) "If the answer is that you'd cash out, then do it now and avoid the misery that is sure to come later." (S035) Sources: 8. High.

9. **Buy more of a company you know when it drops for reasons outside the fundamentals.** "Once I get to know a company, I look for the chance to buy moreshares when the price happens to drop for reasons that have nothingto do with the fundamentals." (S030) "But if I like the stock at 12 and it goes to 9, I'll just buy more you have to understand that and that's the key" (S061) Sources: 7. High.

10. **Insiders buying at your price is a positive; insiders cashing out is a warning.** "When several insiders are buying the company's stock at the same time, it's a positive." (S036) "Whenever I see insiders using the IPO as an excuse to cash out, I ask myself: If they have no faith in the future of their company, why should I?" (S003) Sources: 6 (S003, S014, S029, S036, S090, S091). High. Note: in every source it is one item on a checklist, never the whole case (inferred from S029, S036, S090).

11. **Cheap is not the same as a good story.** "Cheap is different from a [good] story." (S084) "It's always darkest before pitch black. Wait until something's gotten better." (S084) Sources: 5 (S019, S061, S084, S089, S114). High.

12. **Turnarounds need evidence and solvency, not hope.** "So just don't buy on the hope. Wait for the reality." (S046) "does this company have a reasonable chance of turning around, do they have good products, and are they going to be solvent?" (S089) Sources: 6. High.

13. **Bad business news is a reason to research, not to buy.** "people buying stocks because business is bad. It just doesn't work." (S061) Sources: 2 (S061, S089). Medium.

14. **Avoid long shots and whisper stocks.** "I bought about 25 long shots in my life. I've never broken even on one of them never never and that's the truth" (S061) "Long shots almost always miss the mark." (S073) Sources: 3 (S006, S061, S073). High.

15. **Do the homework you'd do for a refrigerator.** "When they buy a refrigerator, they do homework." (S044) "People go to the library and they they do inquire research on a microwave oven and then they'll go out and spend $10000 on a stock" (S066) Sources: 7 (S044, S061, S066, S086, S114, S122, S048). High.

16. **Liking a product starts research; it does not justify a buy.** "Liking a store, a product, or a restaurant is a good reason to get interested in a company and put it on your research list, but it's not enough of a reason to own the stock!" (S116) "People buy a stock and they know nothing about it" (S079) Sources: 3 (S079, S116, S117). High.

17. **Earnings drive stocks over time.** "I can't say enough about the fact that earnings are the key to success in investing in stocks." (S016) "It's a direct relationship — what happens to a company's earnings and what happens to their stocks." (S089) Sources: 8. High.

18. **Don't overpay for growth.** "Overpriced growth stocks are doubly risky" (S031) "When you way overpay for growth, the risk/reward ratiois highly unfavorable." (S031) Sources: 5 (S028, S031, S032, S035, S046). High.

19. **Be patient; the big gains come in years three and four.** "Many of my most successful stocks didn't begin to pay off until the second, third or fourth year I'd owned them." (S112) "In my investing career, the best gains usually have come in the third or fourth year, not in the third or fourth week or the third or fourth month." (S015) Sources: 5. High.

20. **A few big winners make a lifetime; you can afford mistakes.** "You only need one or two good stocks a decade." (S115) "you can afford some mistakes so long as you find some of the really good ones as well" (S112) Sources: 5 (S073, S112, S114, S115, S045). High.

21. **Own few enough to know each one.** "I think a person should only follow five or six companies." (S115) "it's possible to become an expert in four or five" (S023) Sources: 5 (S023, S036, S062, S073, S115). High.

22. **Sell when the story ends, not on price.** "If the story's intact, you hold on." (S085) "You sell the company that was the growth story when there's no room to grow." (S085) Sources: 5. High.

23. **Don't sell winners to keep losers.** "Selling winners and keeping losers is like cutting flowers and watering weeds." (S083) Sources: 4 (S076, S083, S086, S114). High.

24. **The stock doesn't know you own it; your cost is irrelevant.** "Don't buy or sell based on what you paid. Look at the fundamentals." (S114) "You can't treat it like your grandchildren," (S123) Sources: 3 (S061, S114, S123). High.

25. **No options, no margin.** "Don't buy options, and don't invest on margin." (S036) Sources: 2 (S036, S062). Medium.

26. **Great company in a lousy industry beats a good company in a great one.** "If it's a choice between investing in a good company in a great industry, or a great company in a lousy industry, I'll take the great company in the lousy industry any day." (S017) "growth companies and non growth industries are the winners." (S061) Sources: 4 (S017, S061, S084, S114). High.

27. **Stay flexible across sectors.** "They won't look at a railroad, an oil company, a steel company. They're only going to look at companies growing 40% a year. They won't look at turnarounds." (S080) Sources: 4 (S039, S044, S080, S112). High.

28. **Money you need within two years stays out of stocks.** Paraphrase: a one or two year horizon in stocks is silly (S044, S075, S094). Sources: 3. High.
