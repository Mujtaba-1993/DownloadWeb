# Stories: Peter Lynch

The stories and cases he reuses, from 86 public sources (1980 to 2025). One or two lines each, with the lesson and source ids.

1. **Kaiser Industries, "how much lower can it go?"** As a young analyst he backed one of the biggest block buys on the Amex after the stock fell from 26 to 14; it kept falling. Lesson: a fall is not a reason to buy. (S047, S061)

2. **Dunkin' Donuts.** Held for years, made 10 to 15 times his money, never worried about imports or the economy. Lesson: own what you can understand and track. (S046, S053, S067, S084, S089, S122)

3. **Long shots.** Bought about 25 "if this works" stories and never broke even on one; his ten-baggers were surprises from good companies he simply held. Lesson: avoid the whisper stock. (S061, S073)

4. **Dreyfus in the 1987 crash.** Fell to about $17 a share with about $17 a share in cash. Lesson: crashes hand you net-cash bargains in companies you know. (S046, S067, S089)

5. **Magellan went down in every decline.** In 13 years the market fell 10 percent or more nine times and the fund fell more each time; he did not try to dodge them. Lesson: accept declines, do not time them. (S045, S047, S085)

6. **Loading up on cyclicals in 1981 to 1982.** Bought cyclicals in the worst recession since the Depression; one key to Magellan's record. Lesson: buy cyclicals when earnings and sentiment are worst. (S008, S044)

7. **Chrysler.** Waited for the government-backed cash infusion, bought around $4, made about ten times with less risk; recommended it again on Wall Street Week in 1992. Lesson: in turnarounds, wait for the concrete event. (S010, S019, S067, S089)

8. **Sears, the bottom fishers' trap.** People who bought Sears on the dip in 1974 had nothing to show in 1990. Later (1998) he re-looked at Sears as an asset story. Lesson: famous and fallen is not a turnaround; re-examine familiar names fresh. (S019, S042)

9. **Fannie Mae.** Owned and recommended for a decade from single digits; people sold on every scare while it posted record earnings at 9 times earnings. Lesson: separate valid worries from idle ones; markets make mistakes. (S004, S011, S025, S035)

10. **Bank of America in the financial crisis.** Fell from $18 to $7; he judged it could not fail because it was funded by retail deposits, and made a lot of money. Lesson: in a panic, find the fact that settles the survival question. (S084)

11. **Thrift conversions.** Depositors in hundreds of small towns tripled their money buying their own savings bank's shares; insiders buying at the same price was a key check. Lesson: the local edge plus insider alignment. (S014, S029, S037, S091)

12. **Charlie Silk's 150-bagger.** An amateur who bought debt-free small caps below their cash and held Blockbuster through drops. Lesson: homework and patience beat forecasts. (S015, S030)

13. **CML Group (NordicTrack), "Unbuyer's Remorse."** He let a vague worry keep him out of a tenfold gain he could have checked at the mall. Lesson: kill the bogeyman with research before others do. (S004)

14. **Apple and Nvidia, the ones that got away.** Saw his daughter's iPod and Apple's margins, did no work, missed it. Lesson: when the evidence is in front of you and the balance sheet is clean, do the work. (S045, S081, S082)

15. **L'eggs pantyhose and Hanes.** Noticed through his wife; became his biggest position; worried constantly about the competitor No Nonsense. Lesson: the consumer's edge starts the research. (S044, S081)

16. **Taco Bell.** An early buy nobody else would look at; a story that ran from southern California to everywhere. Lesson: how long is the story, and sell when it has no room to grow. (S044, S085)

17. **Pier 1 in the 1991 recession.** Bought at $7 as rival furniture sellers closed. Lesson: a recession clears out competitors for a strong survivor. (S019, S030)

18. **EDS at 500 times earnings.** Fell from 40 to 3 in 1974 though the business did fine. Lesson: price matters even for great growers. (S046, S061)

19. **Philip Morris.** Wall Street never trusted it, the p/e stayed low, the stock still rose tenfold in ten years on earnings. Lesson: earnings win regardless of opinion. (S031, S088)

20. **The withdrawal-rate correction.** A reader showed his 7 percent withdrawal plan failed in the worst case; Lynch published the fix at 5 percent. Lesson: he changes his mind on evidence, in public. (S028, S032)
