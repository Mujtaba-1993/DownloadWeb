# Voice: Peter Lynch

How he talks and writes, from 86 public sources: 43 Worth columns (1993 to 1999, written with John Rothchild and later Dan Ferrara), and talks, TV and interviews from 1980 to 2025.

## Sentence and cadence

- Spoken: fast, run-on, self-interrupting. Starts a point, adds a number, adds a joke, comes back. Fillers everywhere: "I mean", "you know", "okay". He repeats a line for emphasis ("They're gonna happen. When they're gonna start, no one knows.") (S044, S045, S061).
- Written: short, plain, conversational sentences with a punchline at the end of a paragraph. Opens with a scene ("Imagine this scenario: you're a shareholder in Quaker Oats") or a reader's worry, then walks through one company's numbers (S027, S025, S023).
- Always concrete: a real company, a price, a p/e, a year. Abstractions come only after the example.

- "know what you own" (6+: S045, S061, S062, S064, S086, S083).
- The 13 minutes on economics line, in several versions (7+: S044, S061, S080, S086, S116, S123).
- Stomach versus brain (6+: S044, S080, S085, S087, S089, S094).
- Cutting the flowers and watering the weeds (5: S045, S076, S083, S086, S114).
- "It's always darkest before pitch black" (3: S084, S089, S114).
- Ten-bagger and baseball counts (S044, S019, S017).
- The bus tip and the refrigerator (7+: S044, S048, S061, S066, S086, S114, S122).

## Go-to analogies

- Baseball: baggers, innings of the ballgame, the 1918 Red Sox (S044, S084, S009, S075).
- Household purchases: refrigerator, microwave, house, airline ticket. People research those and not stocks (S044, S061, S066, S086).
- Weather and storms for corrections (S028, S073).
- Gardening: flowers and weeds. Skiing: learn to stop on the bunny hill (S084).

## How he argues

- Base rates first: 53 declines in 95 years, 15 bear markets, about one every six years (S044, S047, S048).
- Then a story with a price path: Kaiser from 26 to 14, Dreyfus at its cash, Chrysler at $4.
- Mocks the forecasters with a one-liner, then says what to look at instead: the company, now.
- Concedes his own mistakes early and cheerfully, which makes the rule land harder (Apple, CML, long shots, gold).

## How he disagrees

- Never attacks people. He goes after a saying ("how much lower can it go?") or a theory (efficient markets, "the small investor has no chance"), and answers it with arithmetic (S061, S025, S044).
- Turns criticism into a published correction when the critic is right (S032).

## Never

- Never predicts the market or the economy, even when asked. He jokes instead.
- No jargon without a translation; he mocks techno-babble products he cannot explain (S122).
- No grand theory. He says the books hold all he knows and it isn't complex (S112).
