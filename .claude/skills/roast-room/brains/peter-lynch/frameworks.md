# Frameworks: Peter Lynch

Built from 86 public sources (43 Worth columns 1993 to 1999, interviews and talks 1980 to 2025). Each entry: what it is, the parts, when to use it, how to score with it (guidance drawn from his own thresholds, labeled as guidance), one verbatim quote, and the sources that use it. Most-used first. Lines marked "inferred from" are this brain's reading, not his words.

## 1. Know what you own (the story)
- **What:** Before buying, be able to say what the company does, why it will earn more, and what could trip it up. If you cannot, the only thing you will react to is the price.
- **Parts:** the business in plain words; the five reasons it will go right (S084); the balance sheet; what could go wrong; written down in a logbook and re-checked.
- **When:** every buy, every add, every sell, and every time the stock drops.
- **Score (guidance):** Pass or fail. Fail if the thesis is "it has fallen a lot" or "it's going up". Pass needs a two-minute explanation a child would follow and at least one fact about earnings and debt (inferred from S045, S061, S084, S122).
- **Quote:** "There is a relationship between the kind of research you do and what happens in the stock market and the first rule I want to cover is know what you own." (S061)
- **Sources:** 30+ (S001, S036, S040, S044, S045, S061, S062, S064, S084, S085, S086, S114, S122).

## 2. The 11-year-old test and the written script
- **What:** If you cannot explain to a child in a minute or two why you own it, you should not. Write the reasons on paper and come back to them.
- **Parts:** one-paragraph story; the reasons it will work; the price is not a reason; review the page when the stock moves.
- **When:** at purchase, and as the check when a stock falls.
- **Score (guidance):** A position without a written reason is unowned research, not an investment (inferred from S036, S045, S083).
- **Quote:** "if you can't explain to an 11-year-old and a minute or less, why you own it, not the sucker's going up." (S045)
- **Sources:** 7 (S036, S045, S062, S074, S083, S084, S086).

## 3. Balance sheet first
- **What:** Check debt and cash before anything else. A company with little debt can survive the bad years; most big losses come from weak balance sheets.
- **Parts (Stock Shop rules of thumb):** debt minus cash at about a quarter of net worth is decent; at or above net worth is weak; debt under 20 percent of capitalization is low; cash should at least cover short-term debt; know what is normal for the industry (banks run higher).
- **When:** every stock, and above all for turnarounds, cyclicals, and anything that has crashed.
- **Score (guidance):** Strong = net cash or debt under 20 percent of capital. Weak = net debt at or above equity. A weak balance sheet vetoes a cheap price (inferred from S046, S020, S073).
- **Quote:** "It's very hard to go bankrupt if you don't have any debt." (S047)
- **Sources:** 25+ (S003, S012, S017, S020, S036, S044, S046, S047, S073, S079, S081, S114, S122).

## 4. The six stock categories
- **What:** Sort every stock by what kind of story it is, because each kind is bought and sold for a different reason.
- **Parts:** slow growers (3 to 15 percent, bought for price and dividend); stalwarts and quality growers; fast growers (check the balance sheet so growth can continue); cyclicals (buy at the bottom of earnings, pick survivors); turnarounds (wait for evidence); asset plays and special situations (cash, spin-offs, hidden assets).
- **When:** first step after "what does it do"; it sets the price you should pay and the exit.
- **Score (guidance):** Name the category. If you cannot, you do not know the story (inferred from S036, S046).
- **Quote:** "Is this a growth play, a cyclical play, or a value play?" (S036)
- **Sources:** 10 (S008, S016, S019, S036, S046, S080, S084, S090, S094, S112).

## 5. Price versus earnings, and p/e versus growth
- **What:** Stock prices follow earnings over time. Judge price by the p/e against the growth rate, the company's own history, and the market.
- **Parts:** p/e about equal to growth is fair; half the growth rate is attractive; twice is unattractive (AAII summary, S090); a p/e of 20 is fine for 25 percent growth (S036); overpriced growth is doubly risky (S031); if a 25-times stock sells at 15, ask why (S046).
- **When:** deciding whether a good story is also a good price.
- **Score (guidance):** p/e under growth rate plus yield = cheap; over twice = walk away unless the story is extraordinary (inferred from S090, S031, S036).
- **Quote:** "A key point to remember is that a p/e of 20 is not too much to pay for a company that's growing at 25 percent." (S036)
- **Sources:** 14 (S016, S025, S031, S035, S036, S040, S046, S088, S089, S090).

## 6. The earnings line and the price line (P and E chart)
- **What:** Plot earnings and price together. A steady E line marks a growth company; a wobbly one marks a cyclical. When P falls to meet a rising E, the stock is getting cheap.
- **When:** quick visual check of quality and price; finding quality growers after a sell-off.
- **Score (guidance):** P above E for years = priced for perfection. P at or below a rising E = opportunity (inferred from S016, S031).
- **Quote:** "When the two lines converge, it means the company's growth rate is equal to its p/e ratioŠa good opportunity to invest in futuregrowth at a bargain price." (S031)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S016, S031, S089).

## 7. Decline arithmetic and the stomach test
- **What:** Declines of 10 percent come about every two years and 25 percent declines about every six; nobody predicts them. The question is whether you can hold and add.
- **Parts:** know the base rate; ask in advance what you will do at minus 10 and minus 20; if the answer is sell, own less now; keep money you need in two years out of stocks.
- **When:** sizing positions, and before any market drop.
- **Score (guidance):** If a 25 percent drop would force a sale, the position is too big or the money is wrong (inferred from S035, S080).
- **Quote:** "I mean stomach is the key organ here. It's not the brain." (S044)
- **Sources:** 15 (S028, S032, S035, S044, S047, S048, S074, S080, S085, S087, S089, S094, S118).

## 8. Buy more of what you know when it drops
- **What:** A decline is the chance to buy companies you already understand, if the story has not changed. It is not a reason to buy something you do not understand.
- **When:** corrections, tax-loss season, sector-wide sell-offs.
- **Score (guidance):** Add only if the story is intact and the balance sheet strong; the drop must be for reasons unrelated to the fundamentals (S030).
- **Quote:** "A correction is a wonderful opportunity to buy your favorite companies at a bargain price." (S032)
- **Sources:** 12 (S007, S030, S032, S046, S047, S061, S067, S073, S083, S089, S114).

## 9. Insiders as evidence
- **What:** Insiders sell for many reasons but buy with their own money because they expect a gain. Several insiders buying at the price you pay is a positive; insiders cashing out in an offering is a warning.
- **Parts:** who bought, how much, at what price versus yours; several at once beats one; check the filings or prospectus; insiders dumping in an IPO is a red flag; spin-offs: check months later whether insiders are buying.
- **When:** confirming a story you already understand. Lynch lists it among positives, never as the whole case.
- **Score (guidance):** Cluster buying at or near your price adds conviction to a sound story; it does not rescue a weak balance sheet or a story you cannot explain (inferred from S014, S029, S036, S090).
- **Quote:** "Corporate insiders are not known to be self-destructive. They don't buy company stock unless they expect to make money on it. So when you pay the same price they do, you're in with the right crowd." (S014)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S014, S029, S036, S090, S091).

## 10. Turnarounds: wait for the evidence
- **What:** Fallen giants fool bottom fishers. Buy a turnaround only when something concrete has improved and the company will clearly survive.
- **Parts:** can it stay solvent; good products; a concrete event (cash infusion, closed bad units, rising sales); doubles and triples, not ten-baggers.
- **When:** any stock down sharply on bad business news.
- **Score (guidance):** No evidence of improvement plus heavy debt = pass, however cheap (inferred from S019, S046, S084, S089).
- **Quote:** "So just don't buy on the hope. Wait for the reality." (S046)
- **Sources:** 9 (S011, S019, S046, S061, S067, S081, S084, S089, S114).

## 11. Net cash per share and the cash-adjusted p/e
- **What:** Subtract net cash from the price to see what you really pay for the business. Stocks below net cash with a real business are something for nothing.
- **When:** crashes, tax-loss selling, overlooked small caps, cash-rich companies.
- **Score (guidance):** Price below net cash with earnings = strong buy candidate; below net cash while burning cash = a clock, not a bargain (inferred from S015, S020, S046).
- **Quote:** "Anytime you buy a stock at less than its cash after subtracting all debt and it has good business, you are getting something for nothing." (S046)
- **Sources:** 8 (S015, S020, S030, S041, S046, S067, S089, S090).

## 12. Losers-list bargain screen
- **What:** From the year's beaten-down stocks, take the debt-free ones, then those selling below cash, then those with earnings, then those under ten times earnings.
- **When:** fall tax-loss season and after a sharp decline.
- **Quote:** "You could start by looking for the companies that have no debt." (S020)
- **Sources:** 3 (S015, S020, S030).

## 13. Valid worries versus idle worries
- **What:** Every stock has worries. List them, separate the real ones from the bogeyman, and check each against facts. Doubt itself creates the gain once it is put to rest.
- **When:** any good company that is cheap because people are scared.
- **Quote:** "There's no such thing as a worry-free investment. The trickis to separate the valid worries from the idle worries, and then check the worries against the facts." (S025)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S016, S023, S025, S063, S088).

## 14. The investor's edge
- **What:** Ordinary people see businesses through their jobs and their shopping before Wall Street does. That edge is a reason to start research, not to buy.
- **Parts:** on-the-job edge; consumer's edge; then the homework.
- **Quote:** "the investor's edge is something you already have, not something you acquire by listening to the latest tips from notable sources." (S001)
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S036, S079, S080, S083, S084, S115, S116, S117).

## 15. Turn over more rocks
- **What:** The more companies you look at, the more mispriced ones you find. Be agnostic about sectors.
- **Quote:** "I always thought if you looked at ten companies, you'd find one that's interesting, if you'd look at 20, you'd find two, or if you look at hundred you'll find ten." (S044)
- **Sources:** 4 (S044, S080, S086, S040).

## 16. Growth means sales, and the innings of the ballgame
- **What:** A real grower grows sales, not just margins. Buy growers in innings three to five; when there are none, go to turnarounds, special situations, cyclicals.
- **Quote:** "If you cannot find growth companies in innings three through five of the ballgame, look at turnarounds, special situations, back at the cyclicals." (S084)
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S075, S084, S085).

## 17. Sell when the story ends
- **What:** Sell for the same reason you bought: when the story is complete, the company has no room to grow, or the fundamentals slip. Not because the price went up or down.
- **Quote:** "Deciding when to sell is exactly the same thing as when you buy it." (S085)
- **Sources:** 6 (S080, S083, S085, S086, S114, S123).

## 18. Cyclical timing
- **What:** Buy cyclicals when earnings and sentiment are at their worst; record earnings in a cyclical are a warning.
- **Quote:** "The best time to get involved with cyclicals is when the economy is at its weakest, earnings are at their lowest, and public sentiment is at its bleakest." (S008)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S012, S016, S017, S034, S046).

## 19. Thrift conversion checklist
- **What:** For banks and thrifts: equity to assets 5 or more, ROA near 1 percent, p/e 10 or less, falling bad loans, and insiders buying at your price.
- **Quote:** "You want to see insiders buying the shares and paying the same price as you." (S029)
- **Sources:** 5 (S011, S014, S029, S037, S091).

## 20. Spin-offs and neglected stocks
- **What:** Spin-offs, low institutional ownership, and no analyst coverage create mispricing; freed managers cut costs.
- **Quote:** "If you find a stock with little or no institutional ownership, you've found a potential winner." (S116)
- **Sources:** 6 (S009, S015, S027, S040, S090, S116).

## 21. Risk-reward and the dangerous sayings
- **What:** Ask what you make if right and lose if wrong; want three to one. Reject the sayings that kill investors: it can't go lower, it always comes back, conservative stocks don't fall, I'll sell when it gets back to my price.
- **Quote:** "What will I make if I'm right, and what could I lose if I'm wrong? Look for a risk-reward ratio of three to one or better." (S036)
- **Sources:** 4 (S036, S061, S114, S045).
