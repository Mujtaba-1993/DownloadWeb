# Books: Peter Lynch

His three books with John Rothchild, described in this brain's own words from public sources that discuss them (reviews, his talks and interviews about them). No book text is reproduced. The Worth column series (1992 to 1999) is his largest body of public writing and is in the corpus directly (S001 to S043).

## One Up on Wall Street (1989)
- **Thesis:** An individual who does homework can beat professionals, because they see companies through work and shopping before Wall Street does, and are free of the institutional rules that keep fund managers out of small, dull, or unloved stocks.
- **Key frameworks (in brief):** the investor's edge (on the job and as a consumer); the six stock categories (slow growers, stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, turnarounds, asset plays); the two-minute story; p/e against growth; the checklist of favorable signs, including dull names, spin-offs, low institutional ownership, insiders buying, and the company buying back shares; "diworsification".
- **Taught in:** S001, S036, S079 (his 2015 correction that buy what you know means research, not a mall visit), S090 (AAII summary of his method), S116, S117, S094.

## Beating the Street (1993)
- **Thesis:** How he actually ran Magellan and picked stocks for the Barron's roundtable, case by case, with the lesson that most of his big gains came from patience in companies he understood and that long shots fail.
- **Key frameworks (in brief):** "Peter's Principles" (short rules such as the crayon test); thrift and S&L checklists; turnarounds and cyclicals with evidence; own a handful you can follow; declines as routine opportunities.
- **Taught in:** S073 (a Fool list of its lessons), S074 (Monitor review), S061 (1993 public talk on the book), S114, S094.

## Learn to Earn (1995)
- **Thesis:** A beginner's guide for young people that ties investing to how businesses and capitalism work: buying a stock means owning part of a company.
- **Key frameworks (in brief):** the history and mechanics of companies and markets; why stocks beat savings over time; the case for teaching investing in school.
- **Taught in:** S075 (Monitor interview), S048 (Prodigy chat while promoting it), S033 (Worth column adapted from it).
